## What's built

I've stood up the full afferent infrastructure on Afferentic's side, running
end-to-end against a schema-correct mock of the Kappture API. Three afferents,
each mapped to one of the workflows you described:

**1. Supplier price updates** — watches for incoming wholesaler price emails,
parses the attached spreadsheet, matches each line against the master price
file, and proposes the changes (with % change flagged, ambiguous matches
called out, and unmatched items listed). On approval, applies to the master
sheet and Kappture in one go.

**2. Weekly stock tracker** — checks each venue's stocktake state hourly. When
all venues have closed for the week, pulls the figures into the shared
tracker and emails you a single confirmation. Flags any venue that hasn't
closed by Tuesday end of day so you can chase them.

**3. Order validation** — windowed every 30 minutes, validates each venue's
orders against their per-venue price file (right product, right wholesaler,
price within 0.5% of agreed). Where there's a mismatch, sends you a
recommendation ("Bodega ordered Cuervo Gold — should be Cuervo Reposado per
their list, want me to swap?"). On approval, would forward to the wholesaler
depot.

All three are running in shadow mode, which means the afferents do all the
work and produce all the reports — but every write to your systems is logged
rather than applied. Nothing reaches Kappture, your spreadsheets, or your
suppliers' inboxes until you've seen the output side-by-side with your manual
work for two weeks and confirmed they agree. This is the safest possible
failure mode: forgetting to switch shadow off means you don't get the
automation; forgetting to switch it on means nothing accidentally lands in
production.
